2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE
Sextant answers "what's that up there?" Point the phone at the sky and it shows the stars, planets, Moon, constellations and satellites in that direction, with the satellite you're looking at moving along its trail. It's for anyone outdoors at night or before dawn (stargazers, campers, hunters, anglers) who sees a light moving across the sky and wants to know what it is. It also lists what's visible tonight, including upcoming passes of the ISS and other bright satellites, so you know when to look.

3. HOW TO USE IT
- Open the app. Location is optional. If you allow it, the sky is drawn from where you stand. If you decline, it's drawn for the main city in the device's time zone.
- Point mode (bottom-right button) follows the phone's motion sensors as you hold it up to the sky. Otherwise, drag to look around and pinch to zoom.
- Tap any object for its detail card.
- Search finds any star, planet or satellite and opens on the objects overhead right now.
- Visible Tonight (moon-and-stars button) lists tonight's objects and satellite passes. Pick a pass and tap "Show at <time>" to see it.
- Satellites appear in the sky view only when the Sun is below the horizon at your location, because only then are they lit against a dark sky. During daytime review, use Visible Tonight and "Show at <time>" to see them.
No credentials or sample files are needed.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
- CelesTrak (celestrak.org): public satellite orbital elements, fetched over HTTPS. A snapshot ships in the app so it works offline on first launch.
That is the only network service. There is no authentication, payment, analytics, advertising or AI service. Everything else is computed on the device from bundled open data: Astronomy Engine (MIT), the HYG star catalog (CC BY-SA 4.0), d3-celestial constellation data (BSD-3-Clause), the ESO Milky Way panorama (CC BY 4.0) and SatelliteKit (MIT).

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app works the same in every region. Only the sky it shows changes, because that depends on your location and the time. The interface is in English.
